00

What this sheet is for

A foundry specimen, not documentation. One measured column: key on the left, the real component in the middle, the reading on the right. Nothing is boxed — separation is rule and air, which is the same instruction the pages themselves follow.

Every sample renders from the live tokens and the live component CSS, and every contrast pair is measured at runtime against the threshold its own rendered size requires. Chapter 01 lists what that measurement caught — this sheet exists to find defects, not to present a finished system.

01

What the sheet caught

Five rules were being broken by work already shipped to the branch. Each was found by reading the rule, not by looking at the screen — which is the point: none of them looked wrong.

spacing D4

was — mobile gutter clamped to 20px

now — 24px floor. Below it a phone view earns “generic app”.

spacing D2

was — sections padded symmetrically

now — bottom-heavy 1:1.5. Reading runs downward, so equal padding reads as bottom-tight and shoves each section into the next.

spacing I1

was — one uniform gap in every text stack

now — a .stack with differentiated relationships. A uniform gap flattens a heading and its lead into a list.

motion I3

was — one easing curve for everything

now — ease-out enters, ease-in exits. The inversion is named as the commonest reason interface motion reads as sluggish.

motion D2

was — scale(1.02) on the card photograph

now — a firming rule. Scale is banned outright: it resamples the frame, and the hover budget is one move.

03b

The pairing, and what it replaced

Instrument Serif + Instrument Sans — cut by one hand from related skeletons, so the pair has a reason to exist. The previous pairing had none: Bodoni Moda and Archivo are different centuries and different bones, and the wordmark is already a script, which made four unrelated voices on one page.

The serif ships one weight and a true italic. That is a constraint and also the reason it was chosen: with no bold to reach for, emphasis has to come from size and from the italic, which is exactly the discipline the claim needs.

rejected

Bodoni Moda + Archivo

No relationship between the faces. “Expensive serif plus safe grotesque” is the default luxury move, and it fought the script wordmark.

rejected

Archivo 800 wide + Archivo 400 — no serif at all

Honest and costume-free, and it follows the 360 Auto Care finding that a missing axis, not a wrong family, is usually the fault. Kept on file: it is the fallback if the serif ever reads as luxury-by-default.

rejected

Grotesque display + serif italic on one word

The mixed-line move. Loses to C only because C gets the same contrast without running two families through every headline.

03c

Elegance, and where it comes from

The inventory settles the register. Aventador SVJ Roadster, 488 Pista Spider, Viper ACR Voodoo II one of thirty-one, GT2 RS Weissach, Senna, Regera, Nevera. Mileage of 38. Prices from $900,000 to $3.5 million. The copy already says Ad Personam and Rare Rosso Mimir. This is provenance, not used cars — catalogue content, closer to a sale of lots than to a dealer's floor.

So the engineered and machine registers were the wrong answer, and so was the earlier reasoning that the display face should shut up. What was right in it survives narrowly: the wordmark is expressive, so the display face must be elegant without being ornamental — it may not compete for the same job.

Three answers, differing by where the elegance is produced. Tested on a real lot rather than on an abstract line, because a lot name is what the page is actually made of.

06

The card, and its loading state

The photograph is the card and the whole card is the target — no border, no fill, no button. The skeleton matches the card's real dimensions, so the swap shifts nothing; it pulses opacity and never sweeps a shimmer, which is the template look.
